Negative Numbers — Ordering and Calculations

Placing negative numbers correctly, adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing with negatives.

What This Is

Negative numbers sit below zero on a number line and appear all over GCSE papers — in temperature, debt, coordinates and algebra. You need to order them correctly and carry out addition, subtraction, multiplication and division with them without losing track of the sign. The single rule that trips students up most is what happens when you subtract a negative: it flips to addition. Get that rule clear and the rest follows.

Key Terms You Must Know

Negative number

Any number less than zero. Written with a minus sign in front: −3, −17, −0.5. On a number line it sits to the left of zero.

Integer

A whole number — positive, negative or zero. Examples: −4, 0, 7. Not a decimal or fraction.

Number line

A straight line with numbers placed at equal intervals. Numbers increase from left to right, so negative numbers are always to the left of zero.

Double negative

When a minus sign appears directly in front of another minus sign — as in − (−4). Two negatives together always become a positive: − (−4) = +4.

Absolute value

The size of a number without its sign. The absolute value of −7 is 7. It tells you how far the number is from zero on the number line.

The Method — Step by Step
Grade 3–7 topic. Negative number ordering appears from Grade 3; calculations with negatives and sign rules reach Grade 6–7 in context.
1

Order negative numbers using a number line — smaller means further left

Draw a number line and mark zero at the centre. Numbers increase as you move right, and decrease as you move left — so every negative number is smaller than every positive number, and among negatives the one with the larger digit is actually the smaller number.

Place each number on the line and read off the order from left to right. For example, ordering −5, 3, −2 from smallest to largest: −5 is furthest left, then −2, then 3. The answer is −5, −2, 3.

Example: Order −3, 1, −5, 0 from smallest to largest. On the number line: −5 is leftmost, then −3, then 0, then 1. Answer: −5, −3, 0, 1.
2

Add negative numbers — adding a negative means moving left

Rewrite the addition so you can see its direction. Adding a positive number means moving right on the number line. Adding a negative number means moving left — it has the same effect as subtracting that amount.

Rewrite the expression by replacing + (negative) with just a minus sign. So −3 + (−5) becomes −3 − 5. Now you have a straightforward subtraction and you move left from −3 by another 5 steps, landing on −8.

Example: −3 + (−5) = −3 − 5 = −8. Start at −3, move left 5, land on −8.
3

Subtract a negative number — two negatives always become a positive

Look for a minus sign directly in front of a bracket containing a negative number, as in − (−8). This pattern always simplifies to addition: − (−8) = +8. The logic is that subtracting means taking away, and taking away a debt makes you richer — two opposites cancel and become a positive.

Rewrite the whole expression with the double negative replaced, then calculate. For −3 − (−8): replace − (−8) with +8, giving −3 + 8. Now move right 8 from −3 on the number line to land on +5.

Example: −3 − (−8) = −3 + 8 = 5. Replace the double negative with plus, then calculate.
4

Multiply and divide with negatives — use the sign rules first, then calculate

Count the negative signs before you do any arithmetic. Two negatives multiplied or divided give a positive result. One negative and one positive give a negative result. These rules hold regardless of the size of the numbers — sort the sign first, then handle the digits.

Apply the rule and then multiply or divide the numbers as normal. For −4 × (−3): both negative so the answer is positive, and 4 × 3 = 12, giving +12. For −12 ÷ 4: one negative, one positive so the answer is negative, and 12 ÷ 4 = 3, giving −3.

Example: −3 × (−5) = +15 (two negatives → positive). −3 × 5 = −15 (one negative → negative).
5

In the event of a multi-step calculation — rewrite the signs before you calculate

When a question combines addition, subtraction and negative numbers in one expression — such as −3 + (−5) − (−8) — rewrite the whole expression by replacing every double negative before touching a calculator or doing any arithmetic. Collect the rewritten version as a single clean line first.

Now collect the negative terms and the positive terms separately. From −3 − 5 + 8: the negatives are −3 and −5, giving −8 total. The positive is +8. Combine: −8 + 8 = 0. Working in two sub-steps like this prevents sign errors that happen when you try to do everything in one jump.

Example: −3 + (−5) − (−8). Rewrite: −3 − 5 + 8. Collect: −8 + 8 = 0.
