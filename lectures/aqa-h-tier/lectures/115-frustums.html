Frustums

Find the volume of a frustum by subtracting a small cone from the large one it came from

What This Is

A frustum is the shape left over when you slice the top off a cone with a horizontal cut. Think of a bucket, a lampshade, or a traffic cone without its tip — wide circular base at the bottom, smaller circle at the top, sloping sides. The key to every frustum calculation is that the shape was once a complete large cone, and someone removed a smaller cone from the top. So the volume of the frustum equals the volume of the large cone minus the volume of the small removed cone. That one idea drives the entire method.

Key Terms You Must Know

Frustum

The solid shape formed when a cone is cut by a horizontal plane parallel to its base, and the top portion is removed. It has two circular faces — a large base and a smaller top — connected by a curved sloping surface.

R (large radius)

The radius of the base circle — the wide bottom of the frustum. This comes from the base of the original large cone and is always the bigger of the two radii.

r (small radius)

The radius of the top circle — the narrow opening at the top of the frustum. This equals the base radius of the small cone that was removed from the top.

H (large cone height)

The full height of the original large cone, measured from the base all the way up to the apex — even though the apex has been removed. You often need to calculate this using similar triangles.

h (small cone height)

The height of the small cone that was removed. Together with H, it satisfies h/H = r/R — the similar triangles relationship that makes the method work.

Similar triangles

Because the cut is parallel to the base, the cross-section of the small cone is geometrically similar to the cross-section of the large cone. This means r/R = h/H — the ratio of radii equals the ratio of heights. This is how you find the missing small cone dimensions.

The Method — Step by Step
Grade 8–9 topic. Every frustum question on Edexcel Higher uses the subtract-the-small-cone method shown below. The similar triangles step is the one students most often miss — it is where the method mark is hidden. The crossover extension shows how questions combine frustums with surface area, or give you the frustum height instead of the full cone height.
1

Visualise the frustum as a large cone with a small cone removed from the top

Draw a sketch of the complete large cone — apex at the top, wide circular base at the bottom. Sketch a horizontal dotted line across the cone near the top: everything above that line is the small cone that was removed, and everything below it is the frustum you need to find the volume of.

Label R on the large base radius and r on the small top radius. The full height of the original cone is H, and the height of the removed small cone is h. The height of the frustum itself is H − h. Write all four letters on your sketch before you do any calculation.

e.g. Large cone: R = 6 cm, H = 12 cm. Small cone removed from top: r = 2 cm, h = 4 cm.
2

Use similar triangles to find the missing small cone dimensions

Because the cut is horizontal and parallel to the base, the small cone and the large cone are similar shapes. Similar shapes have all measurements in the same ratio. Write the ratio r/R = h/H — the radius ratio equals the height ratio — and use it to find whichever dimension is missing.

Substitute the values you know into r/R = h/H and solve for the unknown. Cross-multiply to rearrange: if you know r, R, and H but not h, multiply both sides by H to get h = (r × H) / R. Write this calculation out in full — it earns a method mark.

e.g. r/R = h/H → 2/6 = h/12 → h = (2 × 12) ÷ 6 = 4 cm
3

Calculate the volume of the large cone using V = ⅓πR²H

Write the cone volume formula — V = ⅓πR²H — and substitute the large cone values R and H. Work out R² first, then multiply by H, then by ⅓, then by π. Leave your answer in terms of π at this stage — it avoids a rounding error that would compound when you subtract.

e.g. Vlarge = ⅓π(6²)(12) = ⅓π(432) = 144π cm³
4

Calculate the volume of the small cone using V = ⅓πr²h

Use exactly the same formula V = ⅓πr²h but substitute the small cone's radius r and height h. This is the piece that gets subtracted. Again, keep the answer in terms of π so the subtraction is clean.

e.g. Vsmall = ⅓π(2²)(4) = ⅓π(16) = 16π/3 cm³
5

Subtract: V(frustum) = V(large) − V(small)

Write the subtraction explicitly: V(frustum) = V(large) − V(small). Substitute both π values you calculated and work out the subtraction. Convert to a common denominator if needed — 144π = 432π/3, so 432π/3 − 16π/3 = 416π/3.

Evaluate 416π/3 on your calculator to get the decimal answer. Round to the number of decimal places or significant figures the question specifies — always state the units. This final line earns the accuracy mark.

e.g. V = 144π − 16π/3 = 432π/3 − 16π/3 = 416π/3 ≈ 435.6 cm³
6

In the event of a surface area question — use curved surface + two circular ends

The total surface area of a frustum has three parts: the large circular base (area = πR²), the small circular top (area = πr²), and the curved sloping surface between them. The curved part has area π(R + r)l, where l is the slant height of the frustum itself — calculated as l = √[(R − r)² + h²] using the frustum's own height h.

For the worked example frustum: h = 8 cm, so l = √[(6−2)² + 8²] = √[16 + 64] = √80 = 4√5 cm. Then total SA = π(6²) + π(2²) + π(6+2)(4√5) = 36π + 4π + 32√5 π = π(40 + 32√5) ≈ 350.5 cm². Always square and add — never multiply before squaring.

e.g. l = √[(6−2)² + 8²] = √80 cm → SA = πR² + πr² + π(R+r)l = π(40 + 32√5) ≈ 350.5 cm²

Step 1 Find h using similar triangles Edexcel
STEP 1 — Similar triangles

The small cone and large cone are similar shapes, so their dimensions are in the same ratio. Write r/R = h/H, substitute the known values 2/6 = h/12, and solve. Cross-multiply: h = (2 × 12) ÷ 6 = 4 cm. This method mark rewards the ratio setup — write it out even if the arithmetic looks easy.

r/R = h/H  →  2/6 = h/12  →  h = 4 cm
✓M1
Step 2 Volume of the large cone Edexcel
STEP 2 — V(large cone)

Apply V = ⅓πR²H with R = 6 and H = 12. Square R first: 6² = 36. Then: ⅓ × π × 36 × 12 = ⅓ × 432π = 144π. Leave the answer as 144π — do not round yet. Premature rounding here causes the final answer to be wrong. This step earns the second method mark.

Vlarge = ⅓π(36)(12) = 144π cm³
✓M1
Step 3 Volume of the small cone Edexcel
STEP 3 — V(small cone)

Apply the same formula V = ⅓πr²h with r = 2 and h = 4 (the value you found in Step 1). Square r first: 2² = 4. Then: ⅓ × π × 4 × 4 = 16π/3. Again, leave this in terms of π. This is the piece that you will subtract from the large cone volume in the next step.

Vsmall = ⅓π(4)(4) = 16π/3 cm³
Step 4 Subtract to find the frustum volume Edexcel
STEP 4 — Subtract

Subtract V(small) from V(large). To subtract 16π/3 from 144π, convert 144π to thirds: 144π = 432π/3. Then 432π/3 − 16π/3 = 416π/3. Evaluate on your calculator: 416π/3 = 435.634… Round to 3 significant figures as the question asks: 436 cm³. This is the A1 line — make sure the units are written.

V = 432π/3 − 16π/3 = 416π/3 ≈ 436 cm³
✓A1
