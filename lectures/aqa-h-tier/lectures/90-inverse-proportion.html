Inverse Proportion

Finding the constant of proportionality when two quantities are inversely related, then using it to calculate unknown values

What This Is

Two quantities are inversely proportional when one gets larger as the other gets smaller — in exact proportion. If you double x, y halves. If you triple x, y becomes one third. The relationship always fits the formula y = k/x, where k is a fixed number called the constant of proportionality. Every Edexcel inverse proportion question gives you one pair of values, asks you to find k, then asks you to find an unknown. The method is the same every single time.

Key Terms You Must Know

Inversely proportional (∝ 1/x)

Written y ∝ 1/x. As x increases, y decreases at the same rate. The product x × y always equals the same constant k. The graph is a curve that never touches either axis.

Constant of proportionality (k)

The fixed number that makes the equation work for every pair of values. For inverse proportion, k = x × y. Once found, k stays the same for all values in that relationship.

Formula y = k/x

The equation connecting x and y in inverse proportion. You substitute the known x and y values to find k, then substitute k back to find any unknown value.

Direct vs inverse proportion

Direct proportion: y = kx — both increase together. Inverse proportion: y = k/x — one increases as the other decreases. Confusing these two is the most common exam error.

Inverse square proportion (∝ 1/x²)

Written y ∝ 1/x². The formula becomes y = k/x². If x doubles, y becomes one quarter (not one half). Tested at the top of the grade range alongside standard inverse proportion.

The Method — Step by Step
Grade 6–8 topic. Writing y = k/x and finding k is grade 6. Inverse square proportion and finding x from y is grade 7–8.
1

Read the question and write the proportionality formula

Read the phrase "inversely proportional" and write the proportion statement using the ∝ symbol. The statement y ∝ 1/x means y is proportional to one divided by x. This is the mathematical way of saying "as x gets bigger, y gets smaller at the same rate".

Replace the ∝ symbol with = k to turn the proportion statement into an equation. The formula y = k/x always applies in inverse proportion. This is the equation you will use for every calculation in the question.

Question says "y is inversely proportional to x." Write: y ∝ 1/x, then y = k/x.
2

Substitute the known pair of values into y = k/x to find k

Look at the question for the given pair of values — one value of x and the matching value of y. Substitute both numbers into y = k/x. Write the equation with the numbers in place of y and x, leaving k as the unknown.

Rearrange to find k. Multiply both sides by x (here, by 4) to move x from the denominator to the other side. This gives k = y × x. For inverse proportion, k is always the product of the two known values.

Known: x = 4, y = 6. Substitute: 6 = k/4. Multiply both sides by 4: k = 6 × 4 = 24.
3

Write the complete formula with k substituted

Replace k in the formula y = k/x with the value found in Step 2. Write the formula as a proper equation with a number in the numerator. This formula now works for every pair of values in this relationship and is what the mark scheme rewards as the method mark.

k = 24, so the complete formula is y = 24/x. Write this formula before attempting any further substitution.
4

Substitute the given value to find the unknown

Read the second part of the question to find what is being asked. If you are given x and asked to find y, substitute the x value into y = k/x and calculate. Write the substitution as a proper fraction with k on top and the x value on the bottom.

If the question asks you to find x given a value of y, substitute the y value into y = k/x and rearrange. Multiply both sides by x, then divide both sides by y. The result is x = k/y — the same structure, with x and y swapped.

Find y when x = 8: y = 24/8 = 3. Find x when y = 2: 2 = 24/x → x = 24/2 = 12.
5

In the event of inverse square proportion — y ∝ 1/x², use y = k/x²

Edexcel sometimes states "y is inversely proportional to the square of x." This means y ∝ 1/x², which becomes y = k/x². The method is identical — substitute the known pair to find k, write the formula, then substitute the unknown. The only change is that you square x before dividing k by it.

Work through an example: y ∝ 1/x², and y = 4 when x = 3. Substitute: 4 = k/3² = k/9, so k = 4 × 9 = 36. The formula is y = 36/x². When x = 6: y = 36/6² = 36/36 = 1. Square x first, divide into k second — that is the only extra step compared to standard inverse proportion.

y ∝ 1/x², y = 4 when x = 3: k = 4 × 9 = 36, formula y = 36/x². When x = 6: y = 36/36 = 1.

The question gives x = 4 and y = 6. Put 6 in place of y and 4 in place of x in the formula. The equation 6 = k/4 now has only one unknown — k. Write it exactly as a proper fraction with k on top and 4 on the bottom.

Step 3 Find k by multiplying both sides by 4 Edexcel
STEP 3 — Find k

Multiply both sides of 6 = k/4 by 4 to clear the denominator. On the right, 4 ÷ 4 = 1, leaving k. On the left, 6 × 4 = 24. So k = 24. For any inverse proportion question, k is the product of the two known values — you multiply them, never divide.

k = 6 × 4 = 24
✓M1
Step 4 Write the complete formula y = 24/x Edexcel
STEP 4 — Write the formula

Substitute k = 24 back into y = k/x to get the complete formula y = 24/x. Write this formula clearly — it is your tool for every remaining calculation. Some exam papers award a mark specifically for writing the correct formula with k substituted, so always write this line explicitly.

y = 24/x
✓M1
Step 5 Substitute x = 8 to find y Edexcel
STEP 5 — Final answer

Substitute x = 8 into y = 24/x. Write it as the fraction 24/8, then calculate: 24 ÷ 8 = 3. Write y = 3 as the final answer, boxed or underlined. The answer is 3 — and you can verify: 3 × 8 = 24 = k, confirming the inverse proportion relationship holds.
